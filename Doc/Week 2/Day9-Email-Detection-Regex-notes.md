Today, I am writing regex code to detect email addresses.

I am using the regex pattern `\b[A-Za-z0-9._%+-]+@[A-Za-z0-9.-]+\.[A-Za-z]{2,}\b`. This pattern is based on the standard email format: `username@domain.extension`. Here is the explanation: `\b` serves as a boundary marker to ensure matches do not occur in the middle of a word; `[A-Za-z0-9._%+-]+` allows for a combination of uppercase letters (A-Z), lowercase letters (a-z), numbers (0-9), and special characters—specifically the underscore (\_), percent (%), plus (+), and hyphen (-)—for the _username_ section; `+@` ensures at least one character precedes the `@` symbol; `[A-Za-z0-9.-]+` allows for a combination of letters and numbers for the _domain_ section; and `\.[A-Za-z]{2,}` ensures there is a dot followed by at least two letters for the extension section. The backslash `\` is used before the dot (`\.`) because a plain dot in regex has a special meaning (representing any character), so `\.` is required to match the actual dot character. Finally, `{2,}` specifies that the extension section must consist of at least two alphabetic characters.
